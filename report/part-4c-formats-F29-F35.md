# TIER 5 — ENGAGEMENT, TRUST-SPLIT & SYSTEM LAYOUTS (F29–F35)

## F29 · Poll / choice post

**Platform:** FB + IG · **Evidence:** B · **Build time:** ~6 min · **Best KPI:** comments & algorithmic velocity

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★☆☆ · Retention ★★☆☆☆ · Clarity ★★★★☆ · Engagement ★★★★★ · Feasibility · ★★★★★

**Evidence base.** Guardian *Comment is Free* outperforming straight World News (NewsWhip Mar 2016) proves discussion content travels; The Shade Room's community-comments-driven reach (28–29M) shows comment velocity as a ranking input. The poll formalises the "comment 1, 2 or 3" trigger already embedded in F04.

**Structural template (1080×1080):** Navy field; the question as headline (white caps); two choice blocks side by side — `A` and `B` in red-outlined white boxes with the option text; or a native FB poll / IG Story poll sticker variant of the same card.

**Headline archetype:** A3 — fact first, then the binary question: *"The budget raises fuel tax — better budget or worse? A or B."*

**Caption framework.** The fact → the question → the two options as text → *"Vote in the poll / comment A or B."*

**CTA & hashtags.** "Comment A or B" + `#BreakingNews #PakistanNews` (keep to 1–2 tags).

**Canva prompt.** *"1080x1080 news poll poster, dark navy background, white bold question headline, two white boxes with red outlines labelled A and B with short option text, no green, clean professional."*

**Pitfalls.** Polls on tragedies or court cases (tone) · more than two options · polls without a factual base question · overuse (max 1/week).

## F30 · "By the numbers" stat card

**Platform:** FB + IG · **Evidence:** C · **Build time:** ~15 min · **Best KPI:** saves & shares (reference)

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★☆☆ · Retention ★★★☆☆ · Clarity ★★★★★ · Engagement ★★★☆☆ · Feasibility ★★★★☆

**Evidence base.** Directional synthesis: WaPo text-on-colour cards (Adweek 2022) + the market-chart discipline of Bloomberg/CNBC (FB table rows 30/47) — numbers graphics win when they are sourced, and destroy trust when they are wrong. Occasional format only, one verified number at a time.

**Structural template (1080×1080):** Navy or red field; **3 stats max**, each: giant numeral (120 pt, white) + one-line label (28 pt) + thin gold divider; per-stat source in 20 pt; date of data at bottom.

**Headline archetype:** the finding — *"FUEL PRICES UP 14% IN THREE MONTHS"*.

**Caption framework.** The finding → 2 sentences of context → `Source: [authority, date]` → *"Save this for reference."*

**CTA & hashtags.** "Save this for reference" + `#PakistanNews #Explained`.

**Canva prompt.** *"1080x1080 statistics news card, dark navy background, three huge white numbers with short labels, thin gold divider lines, small source and date at bottom, no green, minimal professional."*

**Pitfalls.** Unverified numbers (one wrong figure costs more trust than ten cards earn) · more than 3 stats · mixing years/dates in one card.

## F31 · "What they said vs what it means" dual-panel

**Platform:** IG + FB · **Evidence:** C · **Build time:** ~18 min · **Best KPI:** saves & comments (analysis)

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★☆☆ · Retention ★★★★☆ · Clarity ★★★★★ · Engagement ★★★★☆ · Feasibility ★★★★☆

**Evidence base.** Directional synthesis from the Vox/Axios explainer lineage (F05/F20) and quote-card discipline (F17): the split between verbatim statement and plain-language consequence is the highest-clarity structure for policy/political news, and it separates your analysis from your reporting on the card itself.

**Structural template (1080×1080):** Two stacked panels — top panel navy: `WHAT THEY SAID` + verbatim quote in white (max 25 words, quotation marks, attribution); bottom panel white: `WHAT IT MEANS` + one plain-language sentence in black; red divider; source line.

**Headline archetype:** framing-free — the panels carry it; on-image eyebrow: *"STATEMENT vs IMPACT"*.

**Caption framework.** The quote (attributed) → what it means in 2 sentences (labelled as analysis) → source → *"Save this."*

**CTA & hashtags.** "Save this" + `#Explained #PakistanNews #WorldNews`.

**Canva prompt.** *"1080x1080 split explainer card, top dark navy panel labelled WHAT THEY SAID with white quote text, bottom white panel labelled WHAT IT MEANS with black sentence, red divider, no green, professional."*

**Pitfalls.** Strawmanning the quote · analysis without facts · editing quotes (quote verbatim or don't use the panel).

## F32 · Key-points summary card (live-blog extract)

**Platform:** FB + IG · **Evidence:** B · **Build time:** ~12 min · **Best KPI:** saves & shares during long stories

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★☆☆ · Retention ★★★★☆ · Clarity ★★★★★ · Engagement ★★★★☆ · Feasibility ★★★★★

**Evidence base.** Guardian/Al Jazeera/BBC live-blog "key events" modules (the same source material as F18) — but for stories where sequence matters less than state-of-play. The 5-bullet summary card is the digest form of a live blog.

**Structural template (1080×1080 or 1080×1350):** Navy header: `KEY POINTS — [story], 14:00 PKT`. White body: **5 bullets max**, each one line (red bullet markers, black Inter Bold 30–34 pt), thin grey dividers. Footer: `Last updated 14:00 PKT · Source: [outlet]`.

**Headline archetype:** A6 — *"Key points: the Islamabad talks"*.

**Caption framework.** *"Key points — updated 14:00 PKT"* → the 5 bullets as text → source → *"Save this — we will update it."*

**CTA & hashtags.** "Save this — we will update it" + `#BreakingNews #Explained`.

**Canva prompt.** *"1080x1080 key points news card, dark navy header with KEY POINTS and time, white body with five short bullet lines and red bullet markers, last updated line at bottom, no green, clean professional."*

**Pitfalls.** Bullets over 12 words · stale "last updated" stamps (repost as UPDATE, F23) · duplicating F18 on the same story.

## F33 · Facebook plain-text news note

**Platform:** FB · **Evidence:** A · **Build time:** ~3 min · **Best KPI:** reach per minute invested

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★☆☆ · Retention ★★☆☆☆ · Clarity ★★★★☆ · Engagement ★★★☆☆ · Feasibility ★★★★★

**Evidence base.** Buffer/FrameOS 45M+ posts (2026): Facebook text-only median engagement 4.76% — statistically level with images (5.20%) and above links (4.43%). Facebook's own algorithm has historically given text posts strong organic distribution when they spark comments.

**Structural template.** No image. Plain caption only, structured:

```
HEADLINE (caps line)
One sentence of verified fact.
Source: [outlet]
One CTA.
```

**Headline archetype:** A13 agency-plain or A1 — factual, no ornament.

**Caption framework.** As above, total ≤60 words. Ideal for: quick confirmations, corrections pointers, schedule changes, "we are working on a full card for 14:00".

**CTA & hashtags.** One CTA; `#BreakingNews` max 1 tag.

**Canva prompt.** None.

**Pitfalls.** Long essays in text posts (use F26) · using text posts for photo-worthy stories (F01 wins there) · unformatted walls of text.

## F34 · Instagram grid layout system — the profile-level layout

**Platform:** IG · **Evidence:** B · **Build time:** design once, then free · **Best KPI:** profile conversion (visit → follow)

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★★☆ · Retention ★★★☆☆ · Clarity ★★★★★ · Engagement ★★★☆☆ · Feasibility ★★★★☆

**Evidence base.** BBC's photo-led grid and its engagement rise when video volume halved (Digiday 2018); WaPo's branded colour-card rhythm (Adweek 2022); Al Jazeera's "carefully curated" card-and-photo alternation (Shorty 2020/2021). The grid is the account's storefront: it is judged in 3 seconds by every profile visitor.

**Structural template — the 3-tile rhythm (repeat forever):**

```
[TILE 1: photo-led card (F01/F10)]   — evidence/visual
[TILE 2: typographic card (F03/F09)] — brand colour block
[TILE 3: carousel cover (F02/F11)]   — depth/saves
```

**Visual rules.** Alternate navy and red typographic tiles so no two colour blocks touch. Every 9 tiles = one visual week; check the 3×3 preview for balance (max 2 photo tiles per row, max 1 red tile per row). Reels appear as navy cover frames (F06 prompt) so the grid stays on-palette. No green anywhere (incl. photo duotones).

**Headline archetype.** N/A — grid discipline governs tile design, not wording.

**Caption framework.** Standard (Part 3.7) per post.

**CTA & hashtags.** Standard.

**Canva prompt.** *"Instagram news grid tile set, three tiles: one photo card with navy band, one solid dark navy typographic tile, one navy carousel cover with red accent, consistent typography, no green, professional."*

**Pitfalls.** Random layouts per post · two identical backgrounds touching in the grid · quote-tile overload · breaking the rhythm for every breaking post (breaking news earns one grid slot, the rest keep the system).

## F35 · Results / standings board — sport & elections

**Platform:** FB + IG · **Evidence:** A · **Build time:** ~15 min · **Best KPI:** shares (fan bases)

**Score profile** — Reach ★★★★☆ · Retention ★★★☆☆ · Clarity ★★★★★ · Engagement ★★★★★ · Feasibility ★★★★☆

**Evidence base.** ESPN/SportsCenter/BBC Sport/Sky Sports result-graphic system (ESPN + SportsCenter 28.0M engagements Feb 2025 — NewsWhip/MediaPost); South Asian TV results-board culture (Geo/ARY/Dunya election and match boards). Extends F07 from a single match to tables, series standings and election tallies.

**Structural template (1080×1080):** Navy field; red `[RESULT]` / `[STANDINGS]` / `[ELECTION BOARD]` bar top; a strict table: rows = teams/candidates, columns = played/won/points OR seats won/lead; numerals in white ExtraBold, row dividers in gold thin rules; `As of 21:00 PKT, 15 NOV` + source at bottom. For a single result, use the F07 layout instead.

**Headline archetype:** A2 or board-as-headline — *"PSL POINTS TABLE AFTER MATCH 12"* · *"ELECTION BOARD — 02:00 PKT"*.

**Caption framework.** The headline fact (leader/series state) → one sentence (what changes next) → source → *"Share with your group."*

**CTA & hashtags.** "Share with your group" + `#Cricket #PakistanCricket` or `#ElectionResults`.

**Canva prompt.** *"1080x1080 sports standings table graphic, dark navy background, red RESULT bar on top, white table rows with team names and numbers, thin gold divider rules, time and source at bottom, no green, professional sports design."*

**Pitfalls.** Wrong numerals (double-check every row — a wrong score destroys trust) · unofficial tallies presented as final (label the time, use `[DEVELOPING]` while counting) · player photos (rights).
