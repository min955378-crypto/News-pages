# PART 5 — HEADLINE PHRASING ARCHETYPES

Fourteen reusable formulas, drawn from the headline styles documented across the 100 accounts. Each gives the skeleton, 2–3 worked examples, and where it is allowed. **Universal rules:** the on-image headline = caption line 1; hard news carries **no exclamation marks** (Dawn house style); no curiosity-gap clickbait (BBC plain-headline standard); headline ≤ 40 words in the caption, 5–8 words on the card.

## Headline engineering rules

1. **The one-breath test.** Read the headline aloud once. If you need a second breath, cut it.
2. **Front-load the news.** The first three words must carry the event ("Earthquake hits…", "Pakistan 180/4…"). Never start with "In a shocking turn…" or "You won't believe…".
3. **Verbs beat adjectives.** "Hits", "cuts", "rises", "collapses", "wins", "approves". Emotive adjectives are tabloid tools — they cost trust on a hard-news post (Dawn's no-exclamation rule generalises: no intensifier in hard news).
4. **Specifics beat abstractions.** Numbers, places, times: "Rain closes 12 roads in Lahore" not "Weather causes chaos".
5. **The label must be true.** BREAKING = urgent + happening; DEVELOPING = unfolding + unconfirmed details; EXPLAINED = context; CORRECTION = you are fixing an error. (Fox/NewsWhip label rule.)
6. **Questions only after the fact** (A3) — never a question in place of the news.
7. **Quotes are verbatim or they are not quotes** (A7).
8. **Case convention:** ALL CAPS on cards (Montserrat ExtraBold), sentence case in captions — the words stay identical.
9. **Banned constructions:** "…you won't believe what happened"; "…and what happens next will shock you"; "[Celebrity] DESTROYS…"; exclamation marks in hard news; "BREAKING" on scheduled events (budgets, matches, court verdicts — use `[RESULT]` / `[LIVE]` only if genuinely live).
10. **The caption re-states the headline as line 1** — verbatim — then answers in ≤40 words: what happened, where, when, source.
